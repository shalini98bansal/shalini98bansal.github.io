01 — My role

I led the overall CAD modeling and subsystem design for Vajra, from concept sketches through to the final assembly, and built and tested the physical prototypes — including a PVC mockup used to validate roll-cage geometry and rider clearance before committing to the final frame dimensions. I also rode Vajra in both the race and endurance events at competition, giving me first-hand feedback on the handling and ergonomics decisions we'd made on paper.

02 — Design rationale

Each major subsystem was chosen through a weighted decision matrix scoring the alternatives against comfort, safety, efficiency, cost, and manufacturability.

Vehicle configuration

Semi-recumbent — scored 5.0 vs. −6.5 (recumbent), 1.5 (upright)

Semi-recumbent won on the strength of its safety and efficiency scores, without giving up as much stability as a fully recumbent layout.

Frame material

Al 6063 — scored 7.5 vs. 5.0 (AISI 1018), −7.5 (Al 6061)

Al 6063 machines easily, extrudes into complex shapes, and gave the strength-to-weight ratio needed for a single-mainframe design.

Drivetrain

Chain drive, rear-wheel powered

Chain drive offered lower transmission losses and easier repair than the alternatives; rear-wheel drive improved traction and weight distribution.

Fairing

Partial — scored 8.0 vs. −4.0 (full fairing)

Partial fairing gave up some drag reduction in exchange for lower manufacturing cost and a better field of view for the rider.

04 — Prototyping & testing

Before committing to final dimensions, key design decisions were validated with physical mockups rather than simulation alone.

Cardboard RPS mockup

Roll-cage clearance

A cardboard cut-out confirmed shoulder clearance and rollbar angle with the rider seated.

PVC frame model

Frame geometry

A heat-bent PVC pipe model set front fork positioning and overall frame geometry before fabrication.

Fairing pressure rig

Fairing durability

Fairing material was pressure-tested to 1037 N/m² — well above the 218.1 N/m predicted by CFD.
